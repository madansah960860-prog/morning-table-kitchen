Orders

Guest checkout, ordering by voice, and stopping one that has not shipped.

Is there a sign-up step?

No. This shop runs without accounts of any kind, so nothing needs registering and no password ever has to be kept track of. Buying, reading a policy, starting a return: all of it happens as a guest. Your email address gets used to send a confirmation and a tracking number, and that is the whole extent of it.

Can somebody take my order over the phone?

Yes, and nothing costs more that way than it does on the screen. Dial (555) 010-0100 and the basket gets assembled for you while you talk. Before a card is touched, the list of tools and the figure at the bottom are both read out loud, and you have to say yes to them out loud. Afterward a confirmation lands by email regardless, so something in writing always exists to check against. Desk hours are Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern Time (closed weekends and federal holidays).

I want to stop an order I just placed.

Until the box ships, that costs nothing — a call or an email is enough to pull it. Packing is 1–2 business days, so that is roughly the size of the window. No order number needed either; a name and the delivery address find it just as well. Once the box is with the carrier the request turns into a return instead. Still allowed, still 30 days, but then the label comes off the refund unless the tool itself is at fault.

Shipping

Rates, timings, where boxes go, and why nothing here needs special handling.

What are the two shipping rates?

Standard, $6.95, 3–7 business days. Expedited, $14.95, 2–3 business days. Anything over $59 gets Standard for free. One thing to know about both figures: they measure transit, not the whole wait. Packing days come first and are counted in neither estimate.

How long before the box goes out?

The only slow part under our control is packing, and that is 1–2 business days. Weekends and federal holidays are not counted in it, so a Saturday order starts its clock on Monday. A tracking number lands in your inbox before the box physically moves, because the email fires when the shipping label prints and pickup usually trails that by a few hours.

Is anything here too big or too heavy for Expedited?

No, because nothing in this catalog is oversized. The heaviest carton holds the set of three mixing bowls, at three pounds eleven ounces. The bulkiest single object is the electric can opener, nine and a half inches end to end. Every one of the 22 products qualifies for either service, which is why a mixed order never gets split between a fast parcel and a slow one. Not available to PO boxes. Standard goes to a PO box without any trouble.

Which states, and which carrier?

The 48 contiguous states and Washington, D.C. PO boxes by Standard shipping only. No Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, US territories, or APO/FPO addresses yet. Parcel size and your ZIP code decide which of USPS or UPS ends up with the box; it is not something you pick at checkout. No international shipping. No freight service.

Returns

The window, the label fee, and the one thing that stays in your kitchen.

How long is the window, and how fast is the money back?

30 days, and the count opens on the day the box lands rather than the day you ordered, so a slow week in transit costs you none of it. Changed your mind? That counts as a reason, and no better one will be requested. The real bar is condition: unused, back in the packaging it arrived in, every piece and accessory with it. Post it on a prepaid label for $6.95, which comes off the refund, or hand it to any carrier you like and pay them yourself. Money goes back to the card within 5 business days of the parcel getting here, and your bank then takes a week or two to show it. Swaps are not done — send the tool back, take the refund, order the other one.

Why can something used with food not come back?

Because of what food does to a tool. A skillet that has cooked an egg, a bowl that has held batter, a peeler that has been through a sack of potatoes — none of those can go on to another customer as new, and washing does not change it. Putting one back on the shelf would be dishonest, so anything used with food stays in your kitchen. Unused and boxed, everything else comes back without argument. No restocking fee applies to anything here.

Something arrived cracked, or it is not what I ordered.

Then you pay nothing. Send a photograph within 7 days of the box arriving and pick whichever you want: the same tool again, or the money. Either way the return label is free. Separate from a fault, though, are the things wood and metal do on their own — bamboo grain varies board to board, the stoneware speckle falls where it falls, and stainless picks up water spots when it is left to dry itself. Faults look like this: a cracked handle, a grater panel with no bite, a kettle that boils on past its cut-off, shears whose spring has gone. Those we want to hear about.

Payment

Cards, wallets, tax, and the second the money leaves.

How do I pay, and when does the money actually leave?

A single charge, no more. It lands the second you confirm, it is for the figure on the screen at that moment, and nothing recurring sits behind it. The card itself is keyed into Stripe's checkout rather than into this shop — the digits never come here and there is no field here to keep them in. Six ways to pay: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, Apple Pay, Google Pay.

Does tax get added on top?

If your state charges it, yes, and it goes on at checkout against the ship-to address. The figure appears before you confirm, never after, so the number you agree to is the number your bank will show. Where nothing is owed, nothing is added.

The tools

Five tools, the limits printed on their pages, and what one-handed means.

What does "one-handed" mean in the Handling facts box?

That the tool can be worked from start to finish with one hand, with nothing else needed to hold the job steady. The row says Yes, No, or a single short qualifier, and never more. The spring-loaded shears are a Yes, because the spring reopens them and the hand only has to close. The wide-grip peeler is a No, because the other hand has to hold the vegetable. 5 of the 22 products answer No. That is a description of the tool, not a mark against it.

Why should the nonstick skillet not go above medium-high?

Because the three-layer nonstick coating is the thing you are buying, and heat beyond medium-high is what shortens its life. Push it higher and the surface stops releasing, which is its one job. The body is forged aluminum rather than clad steel, so it comes up to temperature faster than a heavy pan and medium-high on this skillet is hotter than medium-high on a thick one. Same reason it stays out from under a broiler, and the same reason metal utensils stay out of it. For a hard sear, use a pan built for searing.

Why does the carbon steel peeler have to be dried?

Its blade is Japanese carbon steel. Carbon steel takes a finer edge than stainless and keeps it longer, which is why it is on there. The trade is that it spots when left wet. Leave it in a dish rack overnight and it will freckle by morning. Hand wash it, dry it at once, and keep it out of the dishwasher. Freckling is carbon steel behaving like carbon steel, so it is not a manufacturing fault and is not covered as one.

Is the rocker knife any use for paring?

No, and it was never shaped to be. The blade is curved and the handle stands upright above it, so the knife is rocked through food with a closed fist and the arm does the work instead of the wrist. That geometry is poor at paring and poor at anything wanting the point of a blade, because the tip barely gets down to the board. Keep a small paring knife beside it. On bread, cooked chicken, and vegetables on a board, the rocker does the job with one hand.

Will the clip-on strainer hold couscous?

No. The holes are three millimeters. Orzo, small peas, and anything larger stay in the pot; couscous, quinoa, and fine grains go straight through into the sink. Use a fine mesh sieve for those. It is also not a colander — it clips to a pot rim from six to twelve inches so the pot never has to be lifted, carried, and tipped, which is the whole point of it, but it will not sit in the sink for rinsing under a tap. Two-handed: one hand on the pot handle, one on the offset tab.

Getting around

Bigger text, keyboard use, and doing the whole thing by phone.

How do I make the text bigger?

A, A+, A++ — a three-way control sits in the rail and moves every size on the page from 18 pixels up to 21 and then 24. Whatever you choose is stored and reapplied before the next page draws, so it will not flash back to small first. Browser zoom is a separate thing and stacks on top of it; wind that far enough and the left rail folds into a bar across the top instead of squeezing the column.

Can the whole site be driven from the keyboard?

Nothing here needs a mouse. Every control takes tab focus, in the order they are laid out, and the focus ring is a three-pixel Enamel Blue outline held two pixels off the element so it never covers the lettering. One tab from the top of a page puts you on a skip link into the content. Nothing on this site is pinned or sticky — not the rail, not a header — so focus cannot end up hidden behind a fixed layer.

I would rather not use the website at all.

Then do not.
