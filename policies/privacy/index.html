Privacy policy

Last updated September 21, 2026

Nobody signs in here. There is no mailing list and no analytics script. What that leaves is a thin record — who ordered, where it went, what was in the carton — and this page accounts for every part of it.

One section is worth more than the rest: Your privacy choices. Advertising lives there, and so do both free ways of switching it off.

The inventory

What follows is the complete list, not a sample of it. Nothing gets collected for a purpose that is missing from this page. And since no part of the site has a login, none of it compounds into a profile across visits.

Not one line of that counts as sensitive personal information under any US state privacy statute. We hold no government ID numbers, no bank credentials, no precise location, no biometric scans, no medical files, and nothing about faith, ancestry, or union membership. Your birth date is never requested.

Every use it is put to

Five uses, six with advertising. There is no hidden seventh.

  • Taking payment, filling the carton, and moving it to the address on the order
  • Emailing you the four messages an order can produce: receipt, tracking link, delay warning, refund confirmation
  • Writing back to you, and handling returns and warranty claims
  • Keeping whatever sales and tax paperwork a business is legally required to keep
  • Spotting fraud and shutting down abuse of the site
  • Placing advertising and measuring whether it worked, which has a section to itself lower down

Your record is never sold. It never feeds a profile. No marketing mail leaves this address, so there is no list here to get onto and consequently nothing to get off.

The four companies that see part of it

Four, no more, and each is given only the fragment its job actually needs.

  • Stripe takes the checkout payment on hosted pages of its own. Four things are needed for that: the amount, the two addresses on the order, an email for the receipt, and a name. Our servers never see the card number. It goes from the browser to Stripe and stops there.
  • USPS and UPS are given a name, an address, and a telephone number if the order carries one. A parcel does not get delivered without those three.
  • Our host and our mail provider carry the pages and the messages. Their ordinary access logs are a side effect of doing so.
  • Google, for the advertising covered in the next section.

Otherwise your record stays here. It moves only where a law, a subpoena, or a court order compels it, or where we need it to enforce the Terms of service. Sell the business and this policy travels with the records, tying the buyer's hands the same way it ties ours.

Cookies, local storage, and what loads with a page

Zero cookies here. Two items go into local storage and nothing else does: the cart, and your text-size step. Both are written to your own device and read back from it. Nothing about either is transmitted to us. Wipe the browser data and both vanish with it.

Nothing here counts your visit. This site carries no analytics package at all, first-party or third-party. That also rules out tag managers, heatmaps, session replay, and any pixel whose job is to notice which part of a page held your attention.

Advertising is one exception and the card page is the other. Every font, icon, stylesheet, and photograph comes off morningtablekitchen.com itself, so opening a page here rings nobody else's server.

Advertising

We buy advertising from Google Ads. The mechanism: Google can be handed an identifier for your visit. That does two jobs. It lets one of our advertisements show again to a browser that has already been round the shelves, and it tells us whether an advertisement ended in a sale rather than a click.

California classifies that as sharing. Its statute calls the practice cross-context behavioral advertising; elsewhere the identical thing gets labeled targeted advertising. No payment is involved under either label. Turning it off costs nothing, happens by either method set out below in Your privacy choices, and leaves your prices and your deliveries exactly where they were.

The rights that come with your record

Texas, Virginia, Colorado, Oregon, Connecticut, and Montana have each passed something close to this, with more states arriving every legislative season. The Californian version is the CCPA, amended by the CPRA.

We apply the whole set to every US customer. Your state is not checked first. Nobody here works out whether a particular statute technically catches a shop this small. Filtering requests by zip code is more effort than granting them.

  • Know and access. Twice in any twelve months, at no charge, we will send back everything held under your name: each item, its origin, why we had it, and every party it was disclosed to.
  • Delete. Ask and the record is erased, apart from bookkeeping the law obliges us to retain. The invoice for a completed sale is the standard example.
  • Correct. Name the wrong value and the right one replaces it.
  • Portability. A copy in a standard machine-readable format, yours to do as you like with.
  • Stop the advertising sharing. Two ways, both in the next section.
  • Limit sensitive personal information. A genuine right that finds nothing to bite on here, because the category is empty in our records.
  • No penalty. Exercise anything on this list and the price stays where it was, the parcel moves at the same speed, and so does everything that happens afterward.

Your privacy choices

Two ways. Both free. Both end in the same place, and we have no preference between them.

1. Global Privacy Control

Global Privacy Control is honored automatically. If your browser emits that signal, or an add-on emits it for you, the browser is opted out the instant it lands here. No form. No email. No proof of identity. Brave, DuckDuckGo, Firefox and the Privacy Badger extension all emit it; Firefox keeps the toggle in its Privacy & Security preferences.

2. Ask

Subject line "Opt out of sharing" to privacy@morningtablekitchen.com, or ring (555) 010-0100 in office hours. Done inside 15 business days. No reason required. No identity check runs on an opt-out.

An agent can file it for you. All we need in that case is written permission carrying your signature.

Making a request, and proving it is you

Whichever is easiest. Write to privacy@morningtablekitchen.com. Telephone (555) 010-0100. Or open the contact page and set the subject to "Privacy request". Same desk either way.

Proving it is you, without paperwork. No accounts exist here, so there is no password to check against. Instead we line up what you tell us with the order on file: the address it shipped to, the email address it went out under, and roughly when it was placed. Those three together settle it. No driver's license, no passport, no photograph of you, no Social Security number. Opening a bigger file on somebody in order to answer a question about their file makes no sense.

Clocks. An opt-out is done inside 15 business days. Access, deletion, and correction run to 45 days. One extension of a further 45 days is available for a genuinely difficult request, and an email giving the reason goes out before the original deadline expires.

How long each record survives

  • Order records and invoices: a seven-year hold, fixed by federal and state tax rules rather than chosen by us.
  • Correspondence: two years, counted forward from whichever message in the exchange came last.
  • Server logs: 90 days, then overwritten.
  • Opt-out records: permanent, deliberately. An opt-out we forgot about is an opt-out reversed.

Everything above except the final line expires and is deleted on schedule. The opt-out register is the one thing kept forever here, and it is kept forever for your benefit.

Security

HTTPS on every page. The card form is Stripe's, served from Stripe's domain, so a card number is neither written to our servers nor routed through them. Order records are open to the handful of people who pack cartons and answer the telephone, and to nobody else.

No system is proof against everything, and we are not going to suggest ours is. The undertaking is smaller and more honest than that. Routine maintenance happens on schedule, and if anything of yours is ever exposed you get notified in whatever form your state's breach statute demands.

Children under 13

Adults buying kitchen tools are the customers here. Children under 13 are not who the shop is for, nothing on it is designed to draw them in, and we do not knowingly hold information from anyone that young. Nobody is ever asked their age.

Should a child's information end up on file regardless, it gets deleted. Parents and guardians who suspect that has happened should write to privacy@morningtablekitchen.com, and it is handled inside the week.

Under 16 means no advertising sharing at all, whatever else this page says. The rule has stood since the shop opened, and we have no intention of changing it.

Revisions, and reaching a person

Rewrite this page and the date at the top changes with it. Anything that cuts into what you can ask of us gets said out loud, in a place nobody could walk past, instead of being tucked into the middle of a clause.
