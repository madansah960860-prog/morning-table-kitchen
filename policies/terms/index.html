Who this binds

Sample Company LLC runs Morning Table Kitchen Goods at morningtablekitchen.com. Everything set out below applies to anyone using the site, and it takes full effect the moment an order goes through.

Disagree with a clause and the answer is to stop, not to order anyway. (555) 010-0100 will walk you through any paragraph on this page. Five minutes spent that way beats a customer bound by wording they skipped.

Who may buy

Be 18 or older. Be capable of signing a contract. Give us a delivery address inside the area we serve: The 48 contiguous states and Washington, D.C. PO boxes by Standard shipping only. No Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, US territories, or APO/FPO addresses yet.

Household quantities, please. A case of the same skillet for resale is a call to make with us first, and an order shaped like somebody restocking a shelf gets that call before it gets a carton.

What a listing says, and what a photograph is

Four measurements run down every product page in the same box: ounces, grip width in inches, whether one hand is enough, and what a dishwasher does to it. Those are verified before a listing goes live and they govern if anything on the page disagrees. Finish and shade shift a little between batches. That is the material, not a defect.

The picture on a product page is of that model of tool. It is not the individual unit we will pack. A line under every image says so. Each one gets swapped for real stock as real stock gets photographed, and monitors disagree about color regardless. If what lands is materially unlike the listing, we count it as a wrong item and the return costs you nothing. See Returns and refunds.

The goods here are kitchen and household items. A listing gives size, weight, material, and the job the tool does. Nothing on this site claims a product does anything for a medical condition, and nothing on it ever will.

Prices, and the one that is wrong

Everything is priced in US dollars, and the number beside a tool is the tool alone. The cart adds shipping where you can see it. The payment screen adds tax, also in view, also before you commit. Then the figure stops moving. No handling line. No service fee. No surprise at the end.

List prices change when they have to. An order already paid for does not change with them.

Obvious errors. A decimal point slips occasionally. When it does you get a call or an email before the carton is packed, and two options: settle at the right price, or void the order and have every cent returned. Quietly billing you above the total you approved, then explaining later, is not something that happens here.

Accepting an order, and stopping one

Submitting an order offers to buy. Accepting it is our confirmation email, and a contract exists only from there — which is also why we can say no beforehand. The reasons are mundane: the last one sold this morning, the address is outside range, the payment fails verification, or the whole thing smells like card fraud. A declined order is charged nothing, and any authorization sitting on the card is released.

Canceling. Free of charge right up until dispatch, by telephone or by email. The handover to a carrier is the hard cutoff. Once that has happened you are looking at a return and its label, not a cancellation.

Payment

Accepted here: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, Apple Pay, Google Pay. The page where a card number gets typed is built and run by Stripe. A full card number therefore never lands on our systems, in transit or at rest.

Confirming charges the card, for the total on that screen and nothing else. No repeating charge exists anywhere on this site. No subscription, no club, no monthly box quietly billing itself.

Paying is your confirmation that the card or wallet is yours to spend.

Delivery, and where the risk sits

A delivery window estimates. It does not promise. The count begins the day a box leaves this building, not the day the order arrives with us. We write before a promised date is missed rather than after, and the message carries either a new date or your money. The Shipping policy lays out the whole sequence.

Ownership and risk pass at delivery. That is the legal position, and it does not push a lost or flattened parcel onto you. Carriers are our conversation. We are yours.

Returns

30 days, measured from the delivery date. A fault is not required. A restocking fee does not exist. The label costs $6.95 when you have simply changed your mind, and nothing when the item was damaged, defective, or not what you ordered. One exclusion covers tools already used with food. The Returns and refunds page holds all of it and is part of this contract.

Warranty

If an item has a manufacturing defect within 90 days of delivery, we replace it or refund it in full, including original shipping.

Claiming takes two things: an order number, and a line about what went wrong. A photograph usually finishes it. Manufacture is the subject — a handle parting from its shaft, a nonstick coating lifting, a spring that stops springing. Outside it: an empty pan left on a lit burner, a blade put through a dishwasher the listing said to keep it out of, and the slow dulling of any edge after a few hundred meals.

State consumer protection law is not displaced by any of this. The warranty adds to it. Where the two conflict, the statute governs.

Intellectual property

Text, photographs, page layouts, and the Morning Table Kitchen Goods name and marks are the property of Sample Company LLC. Do what you like with them privately: print this page for the folder, save a description, keep a copy of anything here. The line is commercial reuse. Lifting our descriptions or images to help sell something takes our permission in writing first.

Using the site fairly

Five things not to do here.

  • run up an order with no plan to settle it, or use somebody else's card
  • harvest the listings or the photography in bulk and put them to commercial use
  • attack the site, flood it, or hunt for pages that were never made public
  • route junk mail through the contact form
  • pretend to be someone else on a call or in writing

Any of those gets the order canceled. Keeping it up ends the relationship.

Disclaimers

The site comes as it is. Servers drop out and typographical errors get through; both get attention, and neither is warranted here in writing. Two things survive that sentence: the warranty written higher up this page, and the consumer protection rights your own state already gives you. Everything else — the implied warranties of merchantability and fitness for a particular purpose included — is disclaimed to the fullest extent state law allows.

Plenty of states cut a disclaimer of that kind down, or refuse it entirely. If yours is one, the paragraph above achieves less than it appears to.

Limitation of liability

Where a cap is lawful at all, ours is the amount paid for the order in question. Nothing above that. Indirect, incidental, and consequential losses fall outside it and are not accepted.

Fraud is not capped. Neither is death or bodily injury arising from negligence, nor anything else a business may not lawfully limit. Several states restrict caps of this type, and where yours does, the cap has no hold on you.

Indemnity

Break this agreement or misuse the site, and any resulting claim or bill against Sample Company LLC is yours to settle. Costs we caused ourselves remain ours.

Governing law, and how a dispute ends

ST state law applies to this agreement. Conflict-of-law rules are excluded on purpose, so nothing written here can send the contract to another jurisdiction.

Step one is a conversation. Ring (555) 010-0100 or write to support@morningtablekitchen.com and describe what went wrong. You get an answer within 1 business day. Nearly everything ends at that point. Fixing a problem costs both sides less than establishing whose fault it was.

When the conversation fails, either side can go to court: a ST court, or small claims if the amount fits. Your state attorney general and any consumer protection office stay available throughout, and nothing on this page gets in the way of that.

This agreement contains no mandatory arbitration clause. It contains no class-action waiver either. Judge, jury, and the option of standing with other people making the same complaint all remain yours.

General

This page plus the shipping, returns, and privacy policies make up everything agreed between us. Nothing said in an email or on a call adds to it. If a court strikes one clause, the rest survives. Letting something go once does not forfeit it later.

The wording gets revised. Your order is bound by whichever version was published the day it went through, and the date at the top of this page marks the last revision.
