Returns and refunds policy

Last updated September 21, 2026

30 days from the delivery date. Deciding against a tool counts as a reason. No restocking fee, no account, and no figure on this page left vague.

One exception runs through all of it: a tool that has already been used with food stays with you. That is set out under What cannot come back.

Outbound rates, transit windows, and what happens when a delivery date slips are all over on the Shipping policy page.

How long you have

30 days, and the clock starts when the parcel lands. Not when the order goes in. Days the box spent on a truck were the carrier's to lose, and they are not taken off your total.

What has to happen inside the window is the call or the email. The box can follow at its own speed. Once a return is agreed you have another two weeks to get it to a carrier, and nobody here is counting those days.

One practical note. A kitchen tool bought as a gift often sits in a cupboard for a month before anyone opens it, and the window runs from delivery either way. Open the carton when it lands, look the thing over, and put it back. That costs five minutes and buys the whole 30 days.

Second thoughts are reason enough

Nothing has to be broken. The handle is thicker than the drawer allows. The pan is wider than the burner. The timer is loud. The mug is heavier full than it looked empty. All fine, and we do not ask for a reason in the first place.

What the condition line means here: Unused, in original packaging, with all parts and accessories. Lifting a tool out of the box and turning it over is not use. Nobody can judge the weight of a skillet through cardboard. What ends a return is a tool that has been put to work — and in a kitchen that has a sharp definition, set out under What cannot come back.

What a return costs, in dollars

Second thoughts cost $6.95. That is the whole bill. A prepaid label goes out to you and its price comes off what we send back. Rather use your own carrier? Do that, pay them yourself, and nothing at all is subtracted. Either way, buy tracking. Boxes that turn up get refunded; there is no working around one that does not.

Broken, faulty, or not the tool you ordered: zero. Free prepaid return label or a free replacement, if reported within 7 days of delivery with photos. We refund the outbound shipping charge in those cases too. Second thoughts do not get that part back.

Restocking fee: none. There is no such charge in this shop. No tool carries one, at any price, for any reason.

Starting a return, step by step

No sign-in, and no reference number to keep track of. Three ways to start one, and the least troublesome is as good as the rest.

  1. Make contact. Dial (555) 010-0100 — the line is open Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern Time (closed weekends and federal holidays). Or send a note to support@morningtablekitchen.com. Or fill in the form on the contact page. All of it lands on one desk.
  2. Tell us which tool. Quote the order number if it is handy. If not, we can find it from your name and where it shipped to. Add one word about why: mind changed, or something wrong. Those two go down different tracks from that point.
  3. Watch your inbox. A prepaid label comes back to you as a PDF, generally inside the same working day, ready to print. Nowhere to print it? Mention that and we will mail a paper one out at our expense.
  4. Box it back up. Use the packaging it arrived in. Put every lid, guard, and spare part back with it. Tape the new label flat across the old one. Then drop it at a carrier counter, or hand it over at the door.
  5. The money leaves. Within 5 business days of the carton arriving here, and you get an email the moment it does.

Refunds and how long they take

Refunded to the original payment method within 5 business days of receiving the return. Banks may take 3–10 more business days to post it.

A refund goes back the way the payment came in: same card, same wallet. Sending one somewhere else is beyond what our processor allows. Store credit is not pushed on anybody as a substitute for money, though you can ask for it.

How the number is built. Take the price of whatever came back, and the tax that was charged on it. Second thoughts then deduct $6.95 for the label, and the outbound shipping charge stays with us. A fault deducts nothing and hands the outbound charge back as well.

Still nothing in the account? Allow a week and a half first. Banks routinely hold a released refund for three to ten working days at their own end. Beyond that, send the order number over and we will pass on the receipt Stripe generated when the money left. A bank will trace on the strength of that receipt, and generally will not without it.

Reporting damage or a fault

Photographs are what we need. One of the tool. One of the outer carton, if the carton is where the damage started. Both inside 7 days of delivery. A dim phone shot is fine; it is the crack being assessed, not the photography.

Keep the damaged tool or put it in the recycling, whichever suits. Nothing is held back pending its return journey. A replacement usually leaves here on the same day the photographs land in the inbox.

Something that fails a month or two in is a warranty matter rather than a damage report: If an item has a manufacturing defect within 90 days of delivery, we replace it or refund it in full, including original shipping. Detail sits in the Terms of service.

What cannot come back

One rule, and it is a blunt one, so here it is in plain words before the reasoning.

  • Kitchen items that have been used with food. Cook in a pan and it has been used. Drink from a mug and it has been used. Chop on a board, boil water in the kettle, stir something with a spoon — used, all of it. A tool in that state cannot be sold to the next customer as new, and we are not going to pass one along as though it were. So anything that has met food stays with you. Washing it afterward does not undo the first part.

The other half of the rule matters just as much. An unused tool still in its packaging goes back like anything else. Take the peeler out, look at the handle, weigh the pan in your hand, check that the timer display is legible from where you stand, then put it back in the box. None of that counts against you. The line is food, not the lid of the carton.

Nothing else in the catalog is restricted. Where a single product ever carries a condition of its own, that condition is printed on its page above the button, not discovered afterward.

Exchanges, and calling an order off

Exchanges. No direct exchanges. Return the item for a refund and place a new order. Counterintuitive, and still faster. Sending a second order out today beats holding it while the first one crosses the country on a truck. Want the ten-inch skillet instead of the eight? Order it now, post the eight back whenever, and the right pan is on your stove inside the week.

Cancellations. Nothing to pay, any time before dispatch. A call or an email does it. Say the word while the carton is still sitting here unsent, and the charge is simply dropped — every dollar of it, shipping included. After the label prints, a return is the route, and the sections above cover that.
