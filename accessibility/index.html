Accessibility statement

Last updated September 21, 2026

Buying a pan should not depend on which input device you happen to use. What follows: the target, four numbers that beat it, the box of four facts on every product page, three things still wrong, and a telephone number that skips the site entirely at no extra cost.

The position

Screen reader. Keyboard, no mouse. Voice. A magnifier. Type cranked up two steps. Each of those has to carry somebody from the front page to a confirmed order, and the sections below say how, with figures wherever figures exist.

No overlay widget sits in the corner of this site, and none is planned. An overlay is a script bolted on at load time that claims to repair the page underneath it. What it does in practice is argue with software a person has already installed and tuned. The work here is structural instead: nested headings, labels bound to inputs, real button elements, and a grid that holds when it is scaled.

Four figures, and the bar they clear

WCAG 2.2 Level AA is what this build targets. Four measurements land above it. They land above it because a plain page makes the better number cheap, not because of heroics.

  • 10.9:1 — Speckle Charcoal body text against white. The AA floor for body copy is 4.5:1. AAA asks 7:1.
  • 48 by 48 pixels — the smallest thing on the site you can press. Version 2.2 accepts 24 by 24. Doubling each edge quadruples the area.
  • 3 pixels — the Enamel Blue outline that follows the keyboard, standing 2 pixels off whatever it marks. Wide enough to find from across a long form.
  • 18 pixels — where body copy starts, against a web default of 16. Line height 1.6, and policy text held near 60 characters to the line.

The Handling facts box

Every product page carries a boxed panel headed Handling facts. Four rows, same four, same order, all twenty-two products:

  • Weight in ounces, filled where filling applies
  • Handle or grip width in inches
  • One-handed use — yes, no, or a short qualifier
  • Dishwasher safe — yes, no, or which part

It belongs on this page because it is an accessibility feature and not only a shopping one. Two tools can be set against each other on four lines, with no prose in between. That is worth something to anybody listening to a page rather than scanning it, to anybody at 400 percent zoom getting four words at a time, and to anybody who would rather have an ounce figure than an adjective.

When the answer is no, the row says no. A tool needing two hands is not a failed tool, it is a different one, and hiding that inside a paragraph costs somebody a trip to the post office.

What the build does

  • Text size: A, A+, A++. One segmented control, three steps — 18, 21, 24 pixels — applied to the whole site rather than one block of it. The setting is stored locally and survives the browser closing. Layout was drawn at 24 first, so nothing collides at the top step.
  • Zoom. 200 percent costs no content. Keep going and columns stack. The page never asks you to scroll sideways to finish a sentence.
  • Keyboard. Every control answers Tab and Enter. Focus order matches reading order. The outline is never switched off. First stop anywhere is a skip link into the main content.
  • The rail scrolls away. Desktop navigation sits in a left column that is not fixed. Nothing floats, so nothing can settle on top of the control you just moved focus to.
  • Static unless you act. There is no autoplay here, nothing counting down, no carousel rotating behind your back, no dialog opening over the page, and no fresh rows appearing as you scroll. A long shelf finishes with a Show more button, and it waits until you press it.
  • No dragging. A price range is two number inputs. A slider handle is a small target that also moves; a text box is neither.
  • Every icon is captioned. A symbol never does the labeling on its own here. The word it stands for is printed right beside it, at full size.
  • Confirmations are spoken. Added to cart, filter applied, quantity changed — each goes through a live region rather than only being drawn on screen.
  • Forms. Labels sit above their fields and remain visible while you type. An error is a sentence, placed beside the field at fault. A rejected submission hands back every character you entered.
  • No CAPTCHA. A hidden honeypot field stops the bots. You will never be handed a grid of blurred street photographs and told to find the motorcycles in it.
  • No account. This site has no login of any kind. Shopping, paying, reading a policy, opening a return — none of it asks for a password, because there is no password to ask for.
  • Reduced motion. The one animation on the site is a brief color fade. Turn the preference on and it stops.
  • Print. A policy page printed from here drops the navigation, keeps the type at reading size, and comes out as something that goes in a folder.

How it gets tested

axe runs against every route on every build. A serious or critical finding fails that build, so nothing gets past it. Then the manual work: the site walked by keyboard, walked again at 400 percent, and the layout inspected at 360, 768, 1024, and 1440 pixels.

Automation finds maybe half the problems that count. Hand testing takes another bite out of the rest. What survives both shows up in email from somebody who hit it, which is what the last section is for.

Three shortfalls

Listed here, because finding them yourself is worse.

  • The card step is Stripe's, not ours. Checkout hands you over to Stripe-hosted pages at the point of payment. Whatever those pages do with your particular assistive setup is beyond anything we can alter. Hit a wall there and (555) 010-0100 will take the order start to finish by voice.
  • Photographs show the type, not your unit. Alt text describes only what the frame contains. Where a picture and the written numbers could disagree, believe the numbers: ounces, inches, and the four Handling facts rows.
  • Filtering wants JavaScript. Turn it off and the full catalog still renders, each product page still reads end to end, and none of the policy wording goes anywhere. You lose the filters. You keep the shop.

Buying by telephone

Every tool in the catalog can be ordered over the phone. (555) 010-0100, Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern Time (closed weekends and federal holidays). Expect a description of the item, all four Handling facts spoken aloud, then the price. We take the order down as you give it, then read the whole thing back — quantities, total, delivery address — and only then ask about payment.

Same price as the website. No surcharge for using the telephone. No minimum order.

Ask, and we will read out whichever part of the site you want on the same call, or drop the full text of a policy into a plain-text email. Either beats squinting at a screen.

Reporting something broken

Report it. "The add to cart button ignores my keyboard" is the ideal version. "Something on the cart page is wrong" is almost as good. Either beats saying nothing.

Quickest way in is the contact page: its subject list carries an entry reading "Accessibility of this website". During office hours (555) 010-0100 will connect you to somebody directly. Overnight, support@morningtablekitchen.com is the one that gets read first thing.

You will hear back within 1 business day with what the investigation turned up. Anything quick gets fixed, and we tell you the day it went live. Anything slow gets a real estimate instead, alongside another route to whatever you were trying to do. That route is normally the telephone.

Ask us about any of this

(555) 010-0100 is answered by a person during Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern Time (closed weekends and federal holidays). Prefer to write? Send it to support@morningtablekitchen.com and the reply comes within 1 business day. Neither route needs a password, and neither does starting a return.
